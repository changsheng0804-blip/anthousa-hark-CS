## The Great Church (AF 21–30)

After the fire of year 19 the south slope of the Golden Horn was still scorched ground. In AF 22 the people who lived there cut a broad street through the ashes and called it the {{r_firebreak}}, so that flames could never again leap from one roof to the next; nine more houses came down to make room for it. A year later the merchants did not rebuild houses on the burned foundations. They put up a covered row of shops instead, the {{market_embers}}.

With the {{aq_great}} now running full, the emperor gave the fourth hill public baths beside the {{church_apostles}}: the {{baths_constantius}}, a domed hall, finished in AF 25. The army raised the {{tower_horn}} above the {{harbor_neorion}}, where the Black Sea grain ships now lie. By the custom of the age the tower should have been brick and marble revetment; the soldiers used rough-cut ashlar. The engine did not reject it, but it recorded the lapse.

The decade's greatest work rose at the city's eastern end. Beside {{church_peace}} the church raised {{church_megale}}, a timber-roofed basilica about 150 m long, longer than any hall in the city and Anthousa's first wonder. Its foundations are blocks from the old Greek wall of Byzas, which came down when the Founder's wall went up; now that wall lies under the Great Church. Forty-four houses were cleared to make room. The Great Church was consecrated in AF 30.

The decade brought no fire, no earthquake and no plague. From this decade on, the dice for disasters no longer depend on what anyone proposes. By AF 30 the city had 7,380 houses and about 66,420 people.

Water is no longer short: capacity stands at 158,000. Grain is close to its limit: capacity is 108,000, and at the present pace the city will reach it within the next decade.
