## Worship Outside the Walls (AF 51–60)

This is the first decade written people-first: look at who lives here, then build. In AF 50 the city held 137,664 people against a food ceiling of 173,202 and a water ceiling of 173,000. The new emperor, Theodosius, came from the west, and he was a Nicene.

In AF 51 the emperor decreed that only the Nicene faith counted, and before the year was out the Arians were put out of every church in the city. In AF 52 a council met in the city and settled the matter. They would not scatter: on feast days they walked out through the Founder's gate and prayed in a yard beyond the wall, the place later called the {{arian_meeting}}.

What the dry hill lacked most was water. The water-carriers of the Xerolophos climbed up from the Cistern of Modestus with every jar, so in AF 54 they pooled their coppers and built the {{cistern_carriers}} on the slope. The first draft set it in the middle of the hillside with no road to it, and the engine rejected it; moved down beside the Mese and the Founder's gate, it stood. The water ceiling rose to 188,000.

The lots burned in the fire of AF 48 were bought up one by one by grain merchants. In AF 55 they raised the {{horrea_alexandrina}} by the Harbor of Theodosius for the Egyptian grain ships. The first draft sat on top of the old Horrea of Theodosius and was rejected; it moved east onto the burned ground. The food ceiling rose to 193,202.

In AF 57 Theodosius laid out the {{forum_theodosius}} on the Forum Tauri, beside Valens' great fountain.

No fire, earthquake or plague struck in the decade. Only 1,332 new houses went up, against 4,900 the decade before. At its end the city held 149,193 people, and water was again the first ceiling to run dry.
