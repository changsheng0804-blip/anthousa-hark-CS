## The Refounding (AF 1–10)

The founder came. Legend says he walked ahead of the surveyors with a spear in his hand and did not stop until he was 2,800 metres beyond the old wall. When his companions asked how much farther he meant to go, he answered: "Until the one who walks before me stops." There the line of the {{wall_founder}} was drawn, and the land inside the walls grew several times over, most of it still gardens and open ground.

The old wall came down at once and its stones went into the new one, leaving only a faint trace on the ground, together with its {{gate_old_thracian}}.

The spine of the city, the {{r_mese}}, set out from the {{milion}}: 25 metres wide, lined with colonnaded shops, running 600 metres west through the oval {{forum_founder}}, where the porphyry {{col_founder}} stood, and on to the {{gate_founder}}. On the southern slope of the headland the terraces of the {{palace_great}} spread toward the sea, with the 450-metre {{hippodrome}} beside them; next to the acropolis the {{church_peace}} became the city's first great church.

To feed the newcomers the {{harbor_julian}} was cut into the southern shore, with the {{granary_founder}} beside it. The city's grain could now feed 76,000, close to the 80,000 rations the emperor had promised. Beside the {{r_mese}}, the {{cistern_founder}} stored rainwater under brick vaults.

In ten years the population rose from 23,000 to 30,900, and new houses lined up along the new streets, with wide empty plots still between them.

The next bottleneck is water: the old aqueduct and the cistern together serve only 43,000.
