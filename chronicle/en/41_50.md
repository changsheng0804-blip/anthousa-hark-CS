## Valens' Water (AF 41–50)

In AF 40 the city held 96,417 people against a food ceiling of 153,202 and a water ceiling of 158,000. At half again per decade, both ceilings would be hit before the decade was out. Valens was not a popular emperor in the city, but he finished the business of water.

In AF 41 the prefect Modestus dug the {{cistern_modestus}} beside the Holy Apostles, an open reservoir to store the Great Aqueduct's winter surplus. The first draft put it at its scholarly site, but that point falls outside our approximate Founder's wall, with no road and no gate, and the engine rejected it; a lane out to it was tried next and rejected too, for crossing the wall without a gate. In the end it moved inside the wall, beside the end of the northern Mese, about 200 m east. The water ceiling rose to 173,000.

In AF 42 the dry seventh hill between the Founder's wall and the old forum quarter was laid out as the {{d_xerolophos}}, and houses soon filled it.

In AF 44 Valens finished the aqueduct's showpiece on the Forum Tauri: the {{nymphaeum_maius}}, a great fountain where the whole city could see the water arrive. It adds nothing to the water ceiling; it only lets people see it.

For grain, the merchants built the {{horrea_troadensia}} behind the Golden Horn docks, finished in AF 46, for the grain ships from the Troad. The food ceiling rose to 173,202.

In AF 48 a fire broke out by the southern harbors and burned 287 houses.

In AF 49, with few Nicenes left in the city, Gregory of Nazianzus preached in a private house; its small chapel was called the {{church_anastasia}}, the Resurrection.

At the decade's end the city held 137,664 people. Water and food both stood a little above 170,000, and the next decade will drain them both again.
