## The Grain Road (AF 31–40)

In AF 30 the city held 66,420 people against a food ceiling of 108,000; at that pace it would go hungry within the decade. This decade all five factions worked on the same question: where would the grain come from?

The first work went on outside the walls. In AF 31 the people carried the {{r_mese}} on west beyond the Founder's gate to join the old road to Thessalonica: the {{r_egnatia}}. In AF 32 farmers broke ground on both sides of it. To the north the slope is gentle, and it became the {{field_north}}, sown with wheat and barley; to the south the {{field_south}} is flatter still, with market gardens and grain strips running down toward the sea. Together the fields cover about 135 hectares.

But fields cannot save this city. An ancient Mediterranean wheat field yielded roughly 400–600 kg a hectare, a person ate 200 kg of grain or more a year, and half the land lay fallow each year: a hectare fed about one and a half people. All 135 hectares together fed only about 200 more people. Anthousa never lived on its own fields. It lived on the sea.

So the merchants dug the {{harbor_theodosius}} on the Marmara shore, finished in AF 33 and large enough to take the whole Egyptian grain fleet. Its true site at Yenikapı now lies under modern landfill, so the map sets it on today's shoreline, about 200 m to the east-south-east. In AF 34 the emperor built the {{horrea_theodosius}} behind it to store the state grain for the city's free bread. The food ceiling rose from 108,000 to 153,202.

Among the fields outside the wall the church raised the {{church_mocius}} over the grave of the martyr Mocius. The first draft set the church in the middle of the fields, more than forty units from the nearest road, and the engine rejected it because nobody could reach it. The church then laid the {{r_mocius}} up from the {{r_mese}}, the northern fields gave up a strip for it, and the church went up.

In AF 38 a fire on the north slope of the fourth hill destroyed 340 houses, though no public building burned. By the end of the decade the city had 10,713 houses and about 96,417 people.

Grain is enough for now. The next limit is water: capacity is still 158,000, and at the present pace the city will reach it within another decade.
