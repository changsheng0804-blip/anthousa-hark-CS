## The Petitions of the Dry Hill (AF 61–70)

The city of this decade is the one the people of the dry hill asked for. In AF 60 the city held 149,193 people against a food ceiling of 193,202 and a water ceiling of 188,000. In the Xerolophos quarter alone lived 4,383 households, 23,912 people; the commoners set out their troubles one by one and handed them to the engine to judge. The four works below all came out of those petitions.

Crowding came first. In AF 60, 469 households on the slope slept ten or more to a house, and 3,373 sons would turn eighteen within the decade with nowhere to raise a roof of their own. of Peter's tavern a neighbour said "the tavern can't hold them any more"; Photios, off the boat from Cappadocia, said "the cisterns here are shallow, but the people are many." In AF 63 they went out through the Founder's gate, laid the {{r_exo}} along the wall, and began the {{d_exokionion}} outside it, on both sides of the Mese. In ten years 531 houses rose on that open ground beyond the wall, more than in any district inside it.

Water came second. The water-carriers' cistern of AF 54 sits at the foot of the hill, and the queue there ran till dark; 742 carrier households served more than twenty thousand people, one household to thirty-two mouths. Sergios, who came from Thrace on a grain ship, went straight to the cistern to hold a place in line and said "in this city water costs more than bread." The families of the upper slope pooled their coppers again for a second cistern halfway up. The first draft asked for the cistern alone, and the engine rejected it: no road within 25 units, so nobody could reach it. So first came the {{r_carriers}}, climbing straight up from the Mese, and 40 houses were pulled down for them; in AF 62 the {{cistern_slope}} went up at the head of the steps, and 15 more came down. The water ceiling rose to 203,000.

The third was the households left without a breadwinner. In AF 60, 615 households on the slope had no grown man and children under fourteen, nearly one in seven. When Athanasios the tavern-keeper died at forty-two, Martha took over the tavern and "not one of the eight children went without"; but not every house had a tavern. In AF 65 the neighbours, with the monks who had taken in their sons, built the {{xenon_xerolophos}} halfway up the slope, where widows bake for the whole street and orphans learn a trade. Thirteen houses were pulled down for it.

The last was faith. More than half the households on the slope are Arian, and for ten years they could pray only in the timber yard beyond the wall, the {{arian_meeting}}. Makrina lost her little son in his first year; "the Arian priests won't come into the city," she said, "even to pray for a child you have to walk out of the gate." In AF 64 they raised the {{church_arian_brick}} outside the Founder's gate, in brick, without pulling down a single house.

No fire, earthquake or plague struck in the decade. The steps, the cistern and the widows' house cost the hill 68 houses, and the hill's houses went from 746 to 724; 1,124 new houses went up across the city, fewer than the decade before, but the ones beyond the wall are new ground. At its end the city held 158,697 people; the food ceiling stood at 193,202, and water now ran more than forty thousand ahead of the people.
