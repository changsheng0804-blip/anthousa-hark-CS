## Water Comes to the City (AF 11–20)

The Founder's city grew thirsty first. The old aqueduct and a single cistern served only 43,000, and 30,888 people already lived inside the walls, with new houses still lining up along the streets. So the emperor ordered the {{aq_great}}: its springs lie in the Thracian hills to the northwest, 62 metres above the sea, and the channel, about 5.8 km long, falls at an even gradient all the way, passes the Founder's wall, and comes down at 53 metres on the third hill.

Water only runs downhill, but the ground is not flat. Wherever the ground lies more than six metres below the channel, the channel has to ride on stone arches: first the 550-metre {{aqb_outer}} outside the city, then a 100-metre {{aqb_middle}} near the wall, and finally, in the valley between the fourth and third hills, the 560-metre {{aqb_valley}}. Together the three arcades run for more than 1,200 metres. The channel ends at the {{nymphaeum_great}}, a fountain house with a reservoir that feeds every public fountain in the city. Within ten years the water the city could support jumped from 43,000 to 158,000.

At the fork of the {{r_mese}}, the {{r_mese_north}} climbs northwest onto the fourth hill, and on its summit the church raised the cross-shaped {{church_apostles}}, meant as the Founder's mausoleum.

The merchants cut the {{harbor_neorion}} in a sheltered stretch of the Golden Horn west of the old landing, with a row of {{warehouse_horn}} behind it; ships from the Black Sea now had a quay of their own, and grain capacity rose to 105,000. The army built the {{barracks_gate}} just inside the {{gate_founder}}.

In AF 19 a fire broke out at the northern end of the Street of the Lamps, on the Golden Horn side, and destroyed 124 houses, though no public building burned. By the end of the decade the city had 4,973 houses and 44,757 people.

Water is no longer the bottleneck. The next limit is grain: at the present pace the ceiling of 105,000 will be reached in a little over twenty years.
