## Old Byzas (657 BC – eve of the refounding)

The settlers from Megara obeyed the oracle: "Build opposite the city of the blind." Sailing up the strait, they found that the people on the eastern shore had left this headland, washed by water on three sides, untouched, and they landed here. Their leader Byzas raised the {{fort_acropolis}} on the highest point of the headland and dug the {{harbor_prosphorion}} on the sheltered Golden Horn side, so that ships no longer had to fight the currents of the strait.

For more than six hundred years the town never left its headland. The {{agora_tetrastoon}}, framed by four colonnades, was its only square. The priests wanted a temple to Apollo at the tip of the point, but no road reached it, and the temple never left the drawing board. The rough-stone {{wall_byzas}} ran from the Golden Horn to the southern shore, with a single {{gate_old_thracian}} facing Thrace. When the Romans came, the {{aq_hadrian}} brought water down from the northwestern heights.

On the eve of the refounding about 23,000 people were crowded inside the walls, with grain enough for 31,000.
